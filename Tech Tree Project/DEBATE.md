Shared childcare and food sharing are not always the same phenomenon—one is rooted in alloparenting, the other in resource distribution. Mutual aid, meanwhile, is so broad as to be nearly meaningless unless we specify its forms.

I recommend restricting “Cooperative Care” to practices of alloparenting and direct assistance in offspring rearing—i.e., behaviors where individuals care for young that are not their own, or aid the injured in ways that directly impact survival and learning. Food sharing, unless strictly tied to child-rearing or convalescence, deserves its own node or at least a clear sub-branch. This prevents the node from ballooning into a vague “nice things hominins did for each other” supercategory.

In sum: keep, but define boundaries ruthlessly—focus on alloparenting and care for the vulnerable, and excise generic food sharing unless it is inseparable from these practices.

## Question 2: Upstream Edges Only

### Round 1

**Historian:**
Obligate upstream node:
- **Group Living (Obligate):** Cooperative care cannot meaningfully exist without stable group living arrangements. The very premise of shared childcare and mutual aid presupposes a social structure where individuals remain in proximity and interact regularly. Archaeological evidence from early hominin sites—such as shared habitation areas and clustered remains—suggests that group living was a precondition for any systematic cooperative care.

Influence upstream nodes:
- **Prolonged Juvenility (Influence):** The extended period of dependency in hominin offspring likely increased the selective pressure for cooperative care, but it is not strictly obligate; group living could exist with shorter juvenile periods, albeit with less impetus for shared care.
- **Food Sharing (Influence):** While food sharing and cooperative care often co-occur and reinforce one another, early forms of food sharing could exist without formalized cooperative care, and vice versa. The archaeological record shows variability in the emergence of these practices.

In sum: Group Living is obligate; Prolonged Juvenility and Food Sharing are influential but not strictly required. If you wish to argue otherwise, produce evidence of cooperative care in the absence of group living—good luck.

**Engineer:**

The extended dependency period likely intensified the need for shared care, acting as a catalyst for its development. Cooperative care would be significantly less effective without prolonged juvenility, as the demand for shared resources and attention would be reduced.

Regarding **Food Sharing**, I agree it's an influential node. Early instances of food sharing could exist independently, but they often facilitated and reinforced cooperative behaviors, providing a framework for mutual aid and care.

In essence, Group Living is essential, while Prolonged Juvenility and Food Sharing are influential catalysts that shaped the evolution of Cooperative Care.
